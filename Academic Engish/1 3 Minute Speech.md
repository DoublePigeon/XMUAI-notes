# How to write 3MT script

## The HOOK

You need someting to grab attention. A common form of this is by starting with a direct question

> *What does your breath say about you?*

While you can also start by **prompting your audience to do something**, like

> *I want you to tip your head all the way back... now swallow. It almost feels impossible , isn't it?*

## Storytelling 

Then with the attention hooked, we are going to tell a story that intrigues our audience.

To begin with, we need a thing called exposition. It's like a setting that helps you and your audience align your thoughts

And, we are going to use word "and" on commonly agreed statements, "but" to indicate conflict or tension, "therefore" to finally indicate the outcome, for example

> *“Cancer is deadly and chemotherapy is one treatment option,  but it comes with a lot of side effects. Therefore , in our research, we load these drugs into tiny carriers called nanoparticles, which can help to reduce their adverse effects.”*

Your 3MT script will obviously need to be longer than two sentences, but the ABT template provides a useful scaffold that you can build from to ensure that your story is compelling.

Plus, we need something more than ABT to spice it up. Effective tools to do that include **Analogy, Humour and character**, 

By using analogy, comparing your topic that might be a little to difficult to understand for non-expert audiences to something we are all familiar with, we can make our topic easier to understand.

Humour is ... humour. puns, jokes, whatever, who doesn't like laughing? But remember humour needs to be used with care, your topic might be too grim to joke about, then you don't havee to do this do devalue your speech.

Introducing your character can be useful to strike strong emotional response, especially if your topic itself can revoke emotional response.

## Goodbye technical jargon

The audience will be super confused when you fill your speech with technical terminologies and jargons, and we need to ensure that we are speaking the same language with our audience - remember most are not expert in the field. 

We need to simplify our languagem, iterating and tweaking your presentation until it makes sense to the mailman, the bus driver, and the neighbour down the road.

Also, make sure to keep your story focused on the **big picture**, rather than getting bogged down in the details and data. Not only will this make your presentation hard to understand, but it’s also more likely to make it B.O.R.I.N.G. and lead to blank stares!

# Finale 

Then, as the presentation comes to a close, the final segment ends by referring back to the story told from the start. Having this callback will bring your story full circle, thus making it engaging.
